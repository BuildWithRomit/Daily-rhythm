The day

9:30 AM → 1:30 AM IST · tick as you go
  • 9:30 AM15 minWakeNow

    Open the window before the phone

    Sit up, drink a full glass of water, let real light in, three slow breaths. The phone can wait ten minutes.

    Same wake time every day, weekends included — it’s what keeps a 1:30 AM bedtime from drifting to 3.

  • 9:45 AM20 minMoveNow

    Twenty minutes on the floor

    Cat-cow, forward fold, a spinal twist, hip openers, squats holding the wall, calf raises on the stairs. Or three songs of dancing badly.

    Getting fitter starts here, not at a gym: same twenty minutes daily for two weeks, then make it harder — more reps, longer walk, a flight of stairs more.

    Consistency you don’t have to travel for is the kind that survives a results week.

  • 10:15 AM30 minEatNow

    Breakfast, sitting down, no screen

    Something with protein in it: eggs, curd with poha, oats and peanut butter, paneer bhurji, chana. Then write the day in three lines on paper.

    Three lines, not twelve. A short list is a list you finish.

  • 10:45 AM90 minDeep work INow

    Hardest thing first — core molecular biology

    Phone in another room, face down, silent. One tab, one notebook, one topic: replication and repair, gene regulation, signalling, immunology, techniques and their controls.

    Rotate the mode: revise a weak unit → work previous-year questions properly → read one paper end to end.

    This block alone is the difference between a waiting month and a month of progress. Protect it like a class you paid for.

  • 12:15 PM25 minBreatheNow

    A break that actually rests you

    Balcony, water, a fruit, clear one small surface, roll your shoulders out. Scrolling for 25 minutes is not this.

    Stand up, look at something far away. Your eyes and your back are also doing a PhD.

  • 12:40 PM90 minDeep work IINow

    Build something for future-you

    One track per day, rotating:

    Skills — Python for handling and plotting data, LaTeX, image analysis, watching technique videos until the controls make sense.

    The seat — shortlist labs at TIFR, IISER and IISc, read a professor’s three latest papers, draft one honest email.

    The second door — regulatory affairs, learned properly: what CDSCO actually does, ICH guidelines, what sits inside a dossier, one real job posting read line by line.

    Interviews reward the person who can say “I read your 2025 paper, and here’s what I’d ask next.” So do job interviews.

  • 2:15 PM45 minEatNow

    Cook simple, eat properly

    Dal-rice-sabzi, khichdi, rajma-chawal, egg curry, one-pot pulao — with something green on the plate. Put a documentary on while you eat if you want; this meal is allowed to be easy.

    Cooking for one gets skipped first. Batch on Sunday so the tired days still get fed.

  • 3:00 PM40 minRestNow

    Off duty, properly

    A 20–30 minute nap, or lie flat with music. No guilt attached — set one alarm for 40 minutes and forget about it.

    Rest is scheduled here so it doesn’t have to steal the evening.

  • 4:00 PM60 minLight studyNow

    The easy academic hour

    Low effort, still counts: tidy your notes, make flashcards, sort the PDF library, an NPTEL lecture at 1.25×, redraw one pathway from memory and check what you missed.

    This is the flex hour. On a heavy day it goes first, and nothing breaks.

    ↳ Skipping this today. Guilt-free.

  • 5:15 PM30 minAirNow

    Chai and the outside world

    Tea, then get out: around the block, the terrace, four flights of stairs, the corner shop. Ten minutes of evening light counts.

    Leaving the house once a day is the quiet thing that keeps living alone from turning into being alone.

  • 6:00 PM90 minHobbyNow

    The part of the day that’s only yours

    Paint, a film, a whodunit, a documentary, a flight tracked across a continent — or thirty minutes on an instrument, since that’s a long game worth starting now. The jar below picks for you on indecisive days.

    Having one thing that isn’t about results is how the waiting stays bearable.

  • 7:30 PM60 minEatNow

    Dinner, then close the kitchen

    Lighter than lunch: roti-sabzi, soup and toast, curd rice, upma, eggs and salad. Soak the dal, set out tomorrow’s breakfast, ten minutes of tidying, lights off in the kitchen.

    Eating well before 9 PM is most of what makes a 1:30 AM bedtime feel fine.

  • 8:45 PM45 minLife adminNow

    The boring hour that buys you calm

    Forms, the one email you’ve been avoiding, bills, laundry, groceries, recharge, ten minutes of putting the room back. Then one look at the results portals — once, then close the tab.

    One check a day, at a fixed time. Refreshing a results page fourteen times doesn’t make it load faster.

    ↳ Fifteen minutes today. Just the urgent one.

  • 9:30 PM45 minPeopleNow

    Call Mom, or Di, or a friend

    A voice, not a text thread. And once a week, the harder version: a proper conversation with someone whose head works nothing like yours — stay past the first disagreement and ask one more question instead of answering.

    Talking about your subject out loud is also interview practice. Convenient.

  • 10:30 PM90 minFreeNow

    Unscheduled on purpose

    A film, a series, the true crime rabbit hole, the novel, absolutely nothing. No box to tick here except this one.

    A day with no unclaimed time in it is a day you’ll quietly start avoiding.

  • 12:00 AM45 minWind downNow

    Dim everything

    Screens warm and low, wash up, skincare, clothes out for tomorrow, water by the bed. Then four lines: one good thing, one worry parked till morning, one thing you’ll start with — and one moment you answered fast today, and what watching instead would have looked like.

    Writing the worry down is how you stop rehearsing it at 2 AM. The fourth line is how observing becomes a habit rather than a resolution.

  • 12:45 AM45 minReadNow

    The thriller in bed, phone across the room

    Paper if you have it, e-reader on warm if you don’t. Anything that isn’t your syllabus and isn’t a feed.

    The phone on the far side of the room is the single highest-return habit on this whole sheet.

  • 1:30 AM8 hoursSleepNow

    Lights out

    Eight full hours, back round to 9:30. This is the block everything else is built on — not the one to borrow from.

    Goodnight, Diya. Tomorrow is a menu again.

why the small days matter
This season

Small, repeated, boring

  • Move every day — without needing a gym to allow it.
  • Eat like someone who matters — protein, something green, three meals.
  • Observe before answering. One extra beat, then speak.
  • Stay curious past disagreement with people who think differently.
This year

Two doors, both good

  • JRF, then a seat — TIFR, IISER, IISc.
  • Or regulatory affairs, walked into on purpose with the vocabulary already learned.
  • Whichever opens, be the candidate who prepared for both.
Further out

The long game

  • A degree or a title that’s properly yours, and upward from there.
  • Fitter — the version built out of ordinary days like this one.
  • More than one instrument. Start with thirty minutes a week.
  • A car. And a city you chose, rather than one you ended up in.

Bits that keep it running

food, joy, and bad days

🍚 Feeding one person well

Cooking for one, honestly

  • Protein at every meal. Eggs, dal, curd, paneer, chana, soya, chicken or fish if you eat it. This is the single change that makes the biggest difference.
  • Sunday batch: one dal, one dry sabzi, boiled eggs, rice. Three days handled.
  • Always stocked: eggs, curd, atta, oats, poha, peanut butter, bananas, onions, potatoes, frozen peas.
  • No-cook nights: curd rice, bread-omelette, upma, or Maggi with two eggs and a handful of spinach thrown in.
  • Three meals, not two. Skipping lunch is what makes 4 PM feel like failure.

🪫 When the day falls apart

The minimum version

  1. Twenty-five minutes on one thing. A timer, one task, that’s it.
  2. One cooked meal and a full bottle of water.
  3. Step outside once, or call one person.

Do those three and the day counts. Tick nothing else. One rough day is data, not a pattern — and if the flat, heavy feeling settles in for a couple of weeks, that’s worth saying out loud to Mom, to Di, or to a counsellor.

Results will say what they say. The day is still yours to spend well.

Go gently, Diya.
